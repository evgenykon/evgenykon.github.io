Every argument with a flat-earther ends in the same place: the same twenty claims, recycled, and
a Wikipedia link nobody opens. So instead of arguing I made a site that puts the claim and the
rebuttal next to each other — **Плоская Земля: доводы и контр-доводы**.

Site: **[evgenykon.github.io/flat-earth](https://evgenykon.github.io/flat-earth/)** ·
Code: **[github.com/evgenykon/flat-earth](https://github.com/evgenykon/flat-earth)**

## Two sides, two reading modes

- **The light side** (`/light/`) — the flat-earther claims. Open a claim and the scientific
  counter-arguments are right there, no extra clicks.
- **The dark side** (`/dark/`) — the attacks on science taken from the primary sources, quoted
  verbatim with attribution. The rebuttals are **hidden** until you press “I want the
  counter-arguments” at the top of the page: you read the argument the way its author meant it,
  and only then flip the switch.

The dark side is built from three books — Rowbotham, *Zetetic Astronomy* (1865); Carpenter,
*One Hundred Proofs That the Earth Is Not a Globe* (1885); Dubay, *200 Proofs Earth is Not a
Spinning Ball* (2014). The texts are public domain, so a script parses them, splits them into
numbered proofs, groups them by topic and attaches topic-specific counter-arguments
(`scripts/generate-dark.mjs`), then translates everything to Russian through a public Translate
endpoint (`scripts/translate-dark.mjs`). Hand-edited files are never touched by the scripts.

## Content is just markdown

Adding an argument is one file: frontmatter (`title`, `claim`, `argument`, `category`, `order`)
plus the counter-arguments in the body. `git push` → GitHub Actions builds → the argument is
live. No CMS, no database.

The site itself is Astro + Tailwind with a custom sand palette (no default theme), deployed to
GitHub Pages from `main`.

## Why bother

Not to convert anyone — the goal is narrower and more useful: to show *why* each claim fails,
with the original wording in front of you. It is also a decent exercise in reading primary
sources: the 19th-century proofs are a fascinating mix of geometry, bad measurements and total
confidence.
